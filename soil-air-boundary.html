WATER AND AIR IN A SOIL COLUMN

How much air must leave as the water front advances?

Hold the surface water depth constant. Compare freely vented air with a pocket that retains all its original gas. Inspect the minimum gas removal needed to avoid an opposing pressure barrier at a deeper front.

A sealed column base does not tell us whether air can escape through the soil. These are declared physical limits, with no predicted elapsed time.

Declare the column and boundary

Depths run downward from the soil surface. Declare a positive initial front; the singular start from zero depth is outside this inquiry.

Atmospheric head is absolute pressure divided by water density and gravity. It is separate from gauge pressure. No atmospheric value is filled in for your experiment.

Offer capillary-head alternatives

The capillary head is an explicit suction assumption at the front. Missing soil properties are not inferred from the column geometry.

Choose a target front

Keep the target between the initial front and the packed base, with a positive gas region remaining.

State the model assumptions

The column is rigid and uniform, with a fully wet sharp front, positive water conductivity and constant ponding. Air begins at ambient pressure and obeys the isothermal ideal-gas law. The connected gas occupies only the unfilled pore space ahead of the front.

There is no connected tubing or other gas volume, residual gas behind the front, dissolution, changing gas composition, or additional gas input. Gas amount can change only by release from this pocket. Water supplied at the surface replaces water entering the soil.

Why ask this question?

Published ponded sand-column experiments report air compression and counterflow. Their sealed base does not establish a fixed gas amount, and their measured pressure changes between air and water regimes. This inquiry adds an explicit gas-pressure limit; it does not fit those records.

Sources: Cheng et al., 2025 and Mizrahi et al., 2016.
